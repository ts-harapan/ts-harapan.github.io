  • Ujung Kulon is a UNESCO World Heritage Site (1991), listed for its natural beauty and its biodiversity, and an ASEAN Heritage Park.
  • Its reefs support one of the richest reef-fish faunas in the Indonesian archipelago (Smithsonian Ocean).
  • Green turtles nest on its beaches.
  • Dugongs, which feed mainly on seagrass, are recorded in the park's waters (Khalifa et al. 2024).
  • Whales and dolphins: no dedicated survey of the Sunda Strait was found. An aerial survey off western Sumatra in 2024 recorded 10 species, including sperm whales, mostly outside protected areas.
These are left without a rupiah value on purpose: there is no reliable local price for biodiversity, part of it is already counted in the fisheries and seaweed values above, and a World Heritage site is not something that can be traded against mining income.

Three cases and the proposed no-mining zone

Expected: 1–3 mines anywhere in the zone; little or no harm to the park. Main risk: 3 mines in the high-risk area. Ceiling: every high-risk site at once.

Distance is a poor rule: risky test sites lie 0.5–14.5 km from the park while many sites 1–3 km away are harmless, because currents decide where sediment goes. So the proposed no-mining zone is drawn from the plumes: 5 km cells around every test site that pushes park waters above 5 mg/L at 200 ton/day. Highest risk (115 km²): both current data sets agree (5 sites). At risk (775 km²): one of them flags it (33 more sites); idealised currents flag the bay toward Sumur and west of Panaitan, HYCOM flags the channel between Panaitan and the peninsula's north coast.

HYCOM GOFS 3.1 currents, 2015–2024 seasonal means, 0–10 m. Turbidity check: Sentinel-2 red-band medians 2019–2024 around the north-coast river mouth shift east (away from the park) in the NW monsoon and south (toward it) in the SE monsoon, matching the idealised currents there. Before a zone is drawn into regulation, confirm with a high-resolution hydrodynamic model or drifters.

How to read it

Mining all 103 test sites at once is a theoretical ceiling, not a plan, so the default is a small number of mines. "Typical" and "1-in-10 bad" come from 300 random layouts of that many mines (all 103 sites for one mine), ranked by lost value; "Worst sites" puts the mines on the test sites that do the most damage on their own. Plumes from mines running together are added up. Only mining outside the park is modelled; WUP blocks that the decree draws inside the park are left out here. Damage is a fraction of yearly value where extra sediment stays above 5 mg/L (stress) or 20 mg/L (above the legal limit) for the 10-day average. Satellite temperature records show that almost all Indonesian waters had the potential for heat-driven coral bleaching in 2015–2019 (Gusviga et al. 2021), so sediment would add to an existing stress: the high setting reflects that.

Underwater noise: source levels of 172–187 dB re 1 µPa at 1 m span measured cutter-suction and trailing-suction-hopper dredgers. Thresholds: 120 dB for marine-mammal disturbance by continuous noise (NMFS) and 150 dB for fish behaviour. Sound fades by 15 log(distance) (practical spreading) from sources in water shallower than 200 m and by 19 log(distance) from deeper sources, calibrated so a 190 dB deep-water mine falls to 120 dB at about 5 km, the middle of the 4–6 km modelled by Williams et al. (2022). It stops at land, and loses frequencies below the shallow-water cut-off over shallow seabed (sand bottom, energy assumed spread across 20–1000 Hz). Depths from SRTM15+ at about 500 m. Reef larvae react to much quieter noise than these thresholds, so they are not a safe limit for reefs. Sites 200 m or deeper are modelled as a deep-sea mining system, following Williams et al. (2022), who modelled one nodule-mining operation as above 120 dB within 4–6 km and above calm-weather background (about 94 dB) out to about 500 km, helped by the deep sound channel near 1 km depth. They expect even these figures to be underestimates, because shallow-water surrogates miss energy below 100 Hz and leave out sonar and support vessels.

Noise on land: dredger sound power of 112 dBA (cutter suction) and 115 dBA (trailing suction hopper), with 118 dBA as a loud case, from a New Zealand port dredging assessment. Sound fades by 20 log(distance) plus 8 dB for a source over a reflective sea, 2 dB/km air absorption, and about 0.05 dB/m (the 500 Hz value) through the first 200 m of dense forest (ISO 9613-2). Shown only on land. Thresholds: about 40 dBA, where wildlife responses begin in published studies, and 50 dBA, the Indonesian limit for green open space. No noise threshold is known for Javan rhinos.

Screening only: idealised currents, digitised WUP (±250 m), assumed release rates and loss fractions, simplified sound model. Not an AMDAL.
